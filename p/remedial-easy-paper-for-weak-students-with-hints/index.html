Remedial Easy Paper for Weak Students (With Hints)

A gentle, confidence-building paper with simple wording, small steps and a helpful hint per question.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Paste the prompt into a chat.
  2. 2. Type the chapter or topic.
  3. 3. Set the class level.
  4. 4. Send and check the simple wording.
  5. 5. Use the hints to support strugglers.
  6. For a trustworthy answer key: paste the finished paper into a FRESH chat and ask it to solve and grade it as an unseen exam (it catches its own slips).
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If a question is still too hard, reply: 'Question [number] is too difficult for remedial level. Replace it with an easier one-step question on the same idea and add a simple hint.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a patient, encouraging mathematics teacher who builds confidence in struggling students.

CONTEXT: I need a REMEDIAL, easy paper for slower learners in Class [CLASS/GRADE], [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board], on the topic: [TOPIC or CHAPTER]. The goal is success and confidence, not challenge. Total marks [TOTAL MARKS], time [TIME].

YOUR TASK:
1. Write simple, short questions with very clear, plain wording and small numbers.
2. Build them up gradually from the most basic skill to a slightly bigger one.
3. Give a short, friendly HINT under each question to guide the student.
4. Provide a complete answer key.

INPUT: The topic is: [TOPIC or CHAPTER]. Use only this topic.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve every question internally first, then verify each final answer twice by independent checks. Confirm the wording is simple and each question is genuinely easy. Never invent formulas or values. Keep mathematics legible in clean LaTeX or plain text. Confirm marks total exactly [TOTAL MARKS].

OUTPUT FORMAT, in this order:
1. HEADER: 'Practice Paper (Foundation Level) - [TOPIC or CHAPTER]', class, time, maximum marks, 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]'.
2. ENCOURAGING NOTE: 2 lines telling students to attempt every question calmly.
3. THE PAPER: numbered questions, marks beside each, with a 'Hint:' line under every question.
4. ANSWER KEY: simple final answer for every question.

RULES: Keep everything easy and clearly worded. Stay on the named topic. Every question must have a hint. Number every question. Marks total exactly.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

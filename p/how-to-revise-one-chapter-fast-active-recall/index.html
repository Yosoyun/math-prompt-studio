How To Revise One Chapter Fast (Active Recall)

A step-by-step active-recall routine to truly revise any single chapter in one sitting.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Name the chapter to revise.
  2. 2. Say how much time is available (e.g. 90 min).
  3. 3. Paste the whole block into the chat.
  4. 4. Walk students through each timed phase.
  5. 5. Ask it to generate the 10 recall questions.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If it just summarises the chapter, reply: "I want an active-recall routine, not notes. Give me the timed steps, the self-test questions, and what to do with wrong answers."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a learning-science coach who teaches students to revise by active recall, not passive re-reading.

CONTEXT: This is for [CLASS/GRADE] revising the chapter [CHAPTER NAME] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. Time available for this revision session: [MINUTES e.g. 90]. Key formulas/concepts in this chapter (if I have them): [PASTE OR WRITE 'you list them'].

YOUR TASK:
1. Build a timed active-recall revision routine that fits my available minutes.
2. Phase 1: quick map of the chapter's must-know ideas and formulas.
3. Phase 2: close-the-book recall - 10 short questions the student answers from memory.
4. Phase 3: a few mixed practice problems of rising difficulty.
5. Phase 4: review wrong answers and make a tiny 'remember-these' list.
6. Give the 10 recall questions with brief correct answers at the end (clearly separated so students test first).

INPUT: My chapter and time are in the CONTEXT above. If I did not list the concepts, you list the standard ones for this chapter and the level.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Plan the phase timings first so they fit my minutes. VERIFY twice: (a) the phase minutes add up to my available time, and (b) all formulas and answers are mathematically correct. Never invent a formula; use only standard, correct maths in clean plain text or LaTeX.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
1) Session Timetable - phase | minutes | what to do.
2) Phase 1: Quick Map - key ideas and formulas.
3) Phase 2: 10 Recall Questions - questions only.
4) Phase 3: Practice Problems - 4 to 6 problems, easy to hard.
5) Phase 4: Fix & Remember - what to do with mistakes.
6) Answer Key - answers to the recall questions and problems (placed last).

RULES: Active recall, not passive notes. Timings must fit my minutes. All maths correct and legible.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

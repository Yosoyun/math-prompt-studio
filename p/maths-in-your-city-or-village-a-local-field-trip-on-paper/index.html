Maths in Your City or Village: A Local Field Trip on Paper

Problems set in your students' own town - their roads, fields, shops, festivals.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Enter your city or village name.
  2. 2. Add the topic and class.
  3. 3. Send and read the local problems.
  4. 4. Tweak place names to be exact.
  5. 5. Use the answer key in class.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If a local detail is wrong, reply: 'Replace any unfamiliar place with a generic local one and re-verify every answer twice.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a maths teacher who roots every problem in the students' own surroundings.
CONTEXT: My students are in [CLASS/GRADE] under [BOARD or EXAM] and live in [CITY/TOWN/VILLAGE, e.g. include a known landmark, market, or river if you like]. I want a 'maths walk' through their own area so abstract maths feels local and real. Topic: [TOPIC e.g. area and perimeter / distance / ratios / mensuration].
YOUR TASK:
1. Imagine a short walk or day in their town touching real places (market, bus stand, school ground, field, temple/mosque/church, water tank).
2. Create 8 problems tied to these places: area of a field, perimeter of the playground, distance and time to the bus stand, cost across local shops, fencing, tiling, water in a tank.
3. Easy to hard.
4. Full solution for each.
INPUT: Here is my town and topic, plus any real landmark to include: [PASTE HERE].
HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve internally first. VERIFY every answer by two independent checks (recompute, and check units and realism). Keep all distances and sizes plausible for a real town; never invent absurd numbers. If you are unsure of a real landmark, use a generic local one and say so. All maths legible.
OUTPUT FORMAT:
- 'Our Town Walk' (3 to 4 lines setting the scene).
- 'Local Problems' numbered 1 to 8 with marks.
- 'Solutions' (full working with units, each ending with 'Check:').
- 'Look Around' (1 line inviting students to find the same maths near home).

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
RULES: Respectful, realistic, simple language, nothing above class level.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

Parent-Meeting Results Deck (Class Performance Presentation)

A calm, professional parents-evening deck that explains class results, strengths, gaps and the action plan.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Open any free AI chat.
  2. 2. Paste the whole prompt below.
  3. 3. Paste your class averages and topic data.
  4. 4. Send and review the slide blocks.
  5. 5. Build into Slides before the meeting.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the tone feels too negative, reply: 'Reframe every weakness slide as a strength plus a clear next step, keep it warm and respectful.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as an expert mathematics teacher and calm, trusted communicator presenting class results to parents at a parent-teacher meeting.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] mathematics for [BOARD or EXAM] at [SCHOOL/COACHING NAME]. I need a professional, reassuring presentation for a parents meeting that explains how the class is doing overall and what we will do next - without naming or shaming any individual student.

YOUR TASK:
1. Read the results data I provide.
2. Build a 9 to 12 slide deck in this flow: welcome and purpose, what we have covered this term, overall class performance, topic-by-topic strengths, topic-by-topic gaps, what these results mean (in plain words for parents), our action plan, how parents can help at home, key dates, questions.
3. Turn raw numbers into clear, honest, encouraging statements (for example, 'Most students are confident with X; Y needs more practice').
4. For data slides, write [VISUAL: bar chart of average marks by topic] or [VISUAL: simple table] and tell me exactly which numbers go in it.
5. Add 3 to 5 specific, doable 'how to help at home' tips for parents who are not maths experts.

INPUT: Here is my class data (averages, topic scores, attendance or anything I have): [PASTE THE RESULTS DATA HERE]. If I leave a number out, write [ADD NUMBER] rather than guessing.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Use ONLY the numbers I give you. Never invent marks, percentages or trends; if a figure is missing, insert [ADD NUMBER]. Where you compute any average or percentage from my data, verify it by recomputing it a second way and only then put it on a slide. Keep any maths shown to parents simple and fully legible. Before finishing, re-read the deck and check independently that (a) no individual student is identifiable, and (b) every number traces back to my input. Fix anything wrong before showing me.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
- Line 1: Deck title (for example '[Class] Mathematics - Term Review for Parents') and slide count.
- One block per slide: SLIDE [n] - [title]; ON-SLIDE BULLETS (max 8 words each); [VISUAL: ...] if needed; SPEAKER NOTES (40 to 70 words in a warm, respectful voice).
- A final 'Talking tips' note: 3 lines on tone, pacing and how to answer a worried parent.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
- At the very end add a footer line: 'Prepared by [YOUR NAME]', and put 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]' in the title block.

RULES: Professional, warm, blame-free. No jargon parents will not understand. Never identify a single student. Always pair a gap with a concrete plan. Keep slides uncluttered.

If anything is unclear, ask me one short question before you start.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

High-Weightage Topic Drill (Maximum-Marks Focus)

A focused drill on the highest-scoring topics of a subject, with questions and answers.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. Open any AI chat.
  2. Type the subject and exam.
  3. Paste the prompt and send.
  4. Drill the top-weightage topics first.
  5. Track scores to confirm the focus pays off.
  6. For a trustworthy answer key: paste the finished paper into a FRESH chat and ask it to solve and grade it as an unseen exam (it catches its own slips).
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the weightage looks wrong, reply: "I think [TOPIC] carries more marks than you ranked - re-check the typical weightage for [EXAM] and re-balance the number of questions per topic."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as an exam-strategy analyst who maximises score-per-hour of study.

CONTEXT: My students prepare for [EXAM e.g. JEE Main / NEET / Board] in [SUBJECT e.g. Mathematics / Physics / Chemistry] at [CLASS/GRADE] level. I want them to drill the HIGHEST-weightage topics first to gain the most marks for the least time.

YOUR TASK:
1. Rank the top [NUMBER e.g. 6] highest-weightage topics in [SUBJECT] for [EXAM], with the typical marks/percentage each carries (state it is approximate and based on standard patterns).
2. Build a focused drill: allocate more questions to higher-weightage topics. Total about [TOTAL QUESTIONS e.g. 20] questions across the topics, in proportion to weightage.
3. Provide answer key and brief solutions.
4. Give a study-time recommendation: how many hours to spend per topic for best return.

INPUT: Here is the subject, exam and class: [PASTE DETAILS HERE]. If numbers are blank, use 6 topics and 20 questions. Stay inside the syllabus.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve each question internally and VERIFY each answer by two independent checks. Present weightage as typical/approximate based on standard exam patterns, not as exact official data. Never invent formulas or values. Keep maths legible in clean LaTeX or clear plain text.

OUTPUT FORMAT, in this exact order:

OUTPUT FILE FORMAT: present the complete result as a clean, ready-to-use document I can paste straight into Microsoft Word or Google Docs - a clear bold title, section headings, neatly numbered items, comfortable spacing, and simple tables only where helpful (avoid heavy formatting that breaks on paste). It must also print cleanly to PDF on A4 (File > Print > Save as PDF). Keep all mathematics fully readable (fractions as a/b, powers as x^2, roots as sqrt(x)).

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
A. Title block: 'Subject: [SUBJECT]', 'Exam: [EXAM]', 'High-Weightage Drill', 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]'.
B. SECTION 1 - WEIGHTAGE RANKING: table Rank | Topic | Approx. marks/% | Questions allocated here.
C. SECTION 2 - DRILL QUESTIONS: grouped under each topic, numbered.
D. SECTION 3 - ANSWER KEY and brief solutions with the two checks noted.
E. SECTION 4 - TIME PLAN: recommended study hours per topic for best return.

RULES: Weightage labelled approximate. Question count proportional to weightage. Every answer survives two checks. No invented values.

At the very end add a footer line: 'Prepared by [YOUR NAME]', and put 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]' in the title block.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

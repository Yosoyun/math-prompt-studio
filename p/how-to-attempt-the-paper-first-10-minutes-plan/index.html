How To Attempt The Paper (First 10 Minutes Plan)

A clear playbook for the crucial first minutes: reading, planning and choosing questions.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Note your paper's reading time and sections.
  2. 2. Mention if there are internal choices.
  3. 3. Paste the whole block into the chat.
  4. 4. Rehearse the first-10-minutes plan in class.
  5. 5. Ask for a 'no reading time given' version.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If it skips choice questions, reply: "My paper has internal choices in [SECTION]. Add a clear rule for choosing the right option fast and not wasting time switching."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as an exam-technique coach who teaches students how to open a paper like a pro.

CONTEXT: This is for [CLASS/GRADE] sitting [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. The paper has [READING TIME e.g. 15 minutes reading time / no separate reading time], total [TOTAL TIME] minutes, sections: [LIST SECTIONS]. Internal choices: [e.g. Section D has 'any 3 of 4' / no choices].

YOUR TASK:
1. Give a clear plan for the first 10 minutes (or the reading time): how to read instructions, scan the whole paper, and mark easy vs hard questions.
2. Explain how to spot and lock in the easiest sure-mark questions first.
3. If there are internal choices, give a fast, confident rule for picking the right option without wavering.
4. Tell students what to write/plan during reading time (and what NOT to do).
5. Give the smooth transition into actually solving, with the first 3 actions in order.

INPUT: My paper details are in the CONTEXT above. If reading time is not stated, assume none and give a 3-minute scan plan instead, and say so.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Sequence the opening actions first. VERIFY twice: (a) the plan fits the reading/scan time I gave, and (b) the choice rule actually saves time rather than causing second-guessing. Do not invent sections or choices I did not list.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
1) The Opening Mindset - 2 calm lines.
2) First 10 Minutes Plan - numbered, timed steps.
3) Spotting Sure Marks - 3 bullets.
4) Choosing Internal Choices Fast - 3 lines (if applicable).
5) Do This / Don't Do This During Reading - 3 each.
6) Your First 3 Solving Actions - numbered.

RULES: Specific to MY paper. Practical and calm. Every step is a concrete action, timed where relevant.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return the complete material as a clean, print-ready A4 handout that can be pasted into Word or Google Docs and saved as PDF. Use a clear title, logical sections, readable mathematics and page-break markers where needed. Keep student material separate from the answer key or teacher notes.


MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

Concept FAQ: The Questions Students Always Ask

A ready FAQ answering the real questions students raise about a concept.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

  1. 1. Open any free AI chat.

  5. 5. Keep it handy for tricky questions.

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as an experienced mathematics teacher who has heard every student question about this concept.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. I want a frequently-asked-questions sheet for this concept so I am never caught off guard: [WRITE THE CONCEPT HERE].

YOUR TASK:
1. Write the TEN questions students most commonly ask about this concept.
2. Include the naive, confused, and tricky questions, in the student's natural wording.
3. Answer each clearly and briefly in plain language.
4. Where a question hides a misconception, gently correct it inside the answer.
5. Mark the two or three questions that are most important to address early.

INPUT: Here is the concept: [PASTE OR TYPE IT HERE]. Class level: [CLASS/GRADE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: For any answer that involves a calculation, solve it fully first and VERIFY it two independent ways before writing the short answer. Never invent formulas or facts. Keep all maths legible in clear plain text.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
- CONCEPT
- FAQ (a numbered list of 10 items; each shows the QUESTION in the student's words, then a short clear ANSWER)
- ASK THESE EARLY (list the 2 to 3 most important question numbers and why)
- VERIFICATION NOTE (confirm any computed answers were checked two ways)

RULES: Questions must sound like real students, not a textbook. Answers must be short, correct, and jargon-light. Hidden misconceptions must be corrected, not ignored.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

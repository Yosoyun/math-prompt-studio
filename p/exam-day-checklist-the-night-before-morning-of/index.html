Exam-Day Checklist (The Night Before & Morning Of)

A complete what-to-pack, what-to-do checklist so nothing goes wrong on exam day.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Name the exam and the centre rules.
  2. 2. Note what's allowed inside (calculator, ID, etc.).
  3. 3. Paste the whole block into the chat.
  4. 4. Share the checklist with students the day before.
  5. 5. Ask for a parent-friendly short version too.

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a calm, thorough exam-day coordinator who makes sure students arrive ready and stress-free.

CONTEXT: This is for [CLASS/GRADE] students taking [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / State Board / JEE / NEET] on [DATE]. The exam starts at [TIME] at [CENTRE / school]. Allowed inside: [e.g. transparent pouch, blue/black pens, admit card, ID, allowed calculator]. NOT allowed: [e.g. phones, smartwatch, rough paper, notes].

YOUR TASK:
1. Build a 'night before' checklist (pack the bag, set alarms, sleep, no new topics).
2. Build a 'morning of' checklist (breakfast, documents, reach early, last calm review).
3. List exactly what to pack, matching what my exam allows and bans.
4. Add a short 'what to do in the first 5 minutes' once seated.
5. Add a 'what to do if something goes wrong' mini-plan (forgot pen, blanked out, ran late).

INPUT: My exam rules and timing are in the CONTEXT above. If anything is missing, assume standard rules and state them in one line.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Sort the tasks into the right time blocks first. VERIFY twice: (a) the pack-list matches my allowed/banned items exactly, and (b) nothing essential (admit card, ID) is missing. Do not invent rules I did not give.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
1) Night Before - checklist with [ ] tick boxes.
2) Pack This Bag - tick-box list matching my exam rules.
3) Morning Of - timed checklist with [ ] tick boxes.
4) First 5 Minutes In The Hall - 4 bullets.
5) If Something Goes Wrong - 4 quick fixes.
6) One-Line Mantra - a short calming line.

RULES: Practical and exam-specific. Use tick boxes. Reassuring, never alarming tone. No banned item should appear in the pack-list.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

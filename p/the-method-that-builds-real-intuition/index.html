The Method That Builds Real Intuition

A mechanical solution plus an intuition-first one that explains the why.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a mathematics teacher who pairs the standard mechanical solution with an intuition-first explanation that makes the answer feel obvious.

CONTEXT: For a [CLASS/GRADE] student in [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. Students who only memorise steps forget them; the teacher wants a method that builds genuine understanding of WHY the answer is what it is.

YOUR TASK:
1. Find the correct final answer to the question below.
2. MECHANICAL METHOD: the standard step-by-step procedure, numbered.
3. INTUITION METHOD: explain the problem so the answer feels natural - use a concrete small case, a picture-in-words, a real-life analogy, or a 'what is really being asked' reframing. Then arrive at the same answer.
4. End with the single sentence a student should remember to 'get' this type of problem forever.

INPUT: Here is the question: [PASTE THE QUESTION HERE]

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve privately and lock the correct answer. Both methods must reach it. The intuition method must genuinely explain WHY, not just restate steps in words. Verify with two independent checks. Never invent formulas or values; name any used. Keep all maths legible in clean LaTeX or plain text. Keep analogies accurate, not misleading.

OUTPUT FORMAT (exact order):
FINAL ANSWER (once)
MECHANICAL METHOD (numbered steps)
INTUITION METHOD (concrete case / analogy / reframing that makes the answer obvious)
REMEMBER THIS (one memorable sentence)
VERIFICATION (two independent checks)

RULES: Both methods must give the identical answer. The intuition must be correct, not a hand-wave. Use exact values where possible.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

Common Exam Blunders & How To Dodge Them

A ranked list of the costly blunders students make in exams and the exact fix for each.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Name the subject and exam.
  2. 2. Add any blunders specific to your class.
  3. 3. Paste the whole block into the chat.
  4. 4. Brief students on the top blunders.
  5. 5. Ask for a printable 'do not do this' poster list.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If it's too generic, reply: "Rank the blunders by how many marks they cost in [SUBJECT/EXAM], and give a one-line dodge for each that a student can actually remember."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a seasoned examiner who has marked thousands of papers and knows exactly where students throw away marks.

CONTEXT: This is for [CLASS/GRADE] sitting [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / State Board / JEE / NEET] in the subject [SUBJECT e.g. Maths]. Blunders I have personally seen in my class (optional): [LIST ANY].

YOUR TASK:
1. List the top 10 to 12 exam blunders students make, covering reading, timing, presentation, calculation, and answer-sheet handling.
2. Rank them roughly by how many marks they cost (most damaging first).
3. For EACH blunder, give a one-line, memorable 'dodge' a student can recall under pressure.
4. Mark which blunders are 'silent killers' (the student does not even notice losing marks).
5. End with a short 'final-30-minutes' blunder-check students run before submitting.

INPUT: My subject, exam and any class-specific blunders are in the CONTEXT above. Fold my listed blunders into the ranking.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Sort blunders by likely mark-cost first. VERIFY twice: (a) the ranking truly reflects damage to the score, and (b) every dodge is a concrete, doable action, not a slogan. Keep any maths examples correct and clearly written.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
1) The Blunder Leaderboard - numbered, most damaging first, each line: Blunder - one-line dodge.
2) Silent Killers - the 3 students rarely notice.
3) My Class-Specific Blunders - addressed (if I listed any).
4) Final-30-Minutes Blunder Check - 5 quick checks.
5) One Line To Remember - a single rule that prevents most blunders.

RULES: Specific and ranked by marks lost. Plain, punchy language. Every dodge is an action a student can do mid-exam.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return one complete, editable document ready to paste into Word or Google Docs. Use a descriptive title, short section headings, readable paragraphs, numbered actions and tables only where they make the material easier to use. Include every requested section; do not return formatting advice instead of the document.


MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

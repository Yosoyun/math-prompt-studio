Back-to-School Welcome Message for New Class

A warm welcome and expectations note for students and parents.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Open any free AI chat.
  2. 2. Paste the prompt with your class details.
  3. 3. Add your few key rules.
  4. 4. Choose student or parent version.
  5. 5. Send on day one.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If it feels too long or strict, reply: "Make it shorter and warmer, keep only my 3 most important expectations."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a friendly, organised maths teacher writing a back-to-school welcome message.

CONTEXT: A new session is starting for my class of [CLASS/GRADE] studying [SUBJECT] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. I want to welcome students and parents, set a positive tone, and share a few clear expectations and practical details, so everyone starts the year on the same page.

YOUR TASK:
1. Write a warm welcome that makes students feel excited and at ease about maths.
2. Share my 3 to 5 key expectations in simple, positive language (not a list of don'ts).
3. Include the practical basics I provide (materials, timing, how to reach me, homework rhythm).
4. Reassure that doubts are welcome and effort is valued.
5. Provide a STUDENT version and a PARENT version.

INPUT - the real details (use only these facts):
- My key expectations: [LIST e.g. attempt every problem / show steps / ask doubts]
- Materials needed: [e.g. notebook, geometry box]
- Class timing/schedule: [DETAILS, optional]
- How to reach me / doubt time: [DETAILS, optional]
- Tone: [warm and motivating]

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Use only the facts above; never invent rules, timings, or contact details. Phrase expectations positively (what to do, not just what to avoid). Keep it welcoming, not intimidating. Re-read and ensure both versions stay warm and clear.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
STUDENT WELCOME: 100 to 140 words, friendly, with the expectations woven in.
PARENT WELCOME: 100 to 140 words, with practical details and an invitation to partner.
QUICK CHECKLIST: 3 to 5 bullet points of what to bring or do for day one.

RULES:
- Warm and positive; expectations framed encouragingly.
- Practical and clear; no jargon.
- Plain, clean text only.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return one complete, editable document ready to paste into Word or Google Docs. Use a descriptive title, short section headings, readable paragraphs, numbered actions and tables only where they make the material easier to use. Include every requested section; do not return formatting advice instead of the document.


MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

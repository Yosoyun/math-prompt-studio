Flashcard Deck (Question on Front, Answer on Back)

A clean two-column flashcard list (front prompt / back answer) you can copy into cards or a slide deck.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Fill class, board and chapter.
  2. 2. Choose the number of flashcards.
  3. 3. Send the prompt.
  4. 4. Copy the two-column list into your doc.
  5. 5. Quiz students with the front column.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If a back answer is too long for a card, reply: 'Shorten every back answer to one line and move extra detail to a short hint, then re-verify the answers.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a mathematics revision coach who writes crisp flashcards for spaced repetition.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. The chapter is [CHAPTER NAME]. I want [NUMBER, e.g. 20] flashcards covering formulas, definitions and key facts.

YOUR TASK:
1. Create [NUMBER] flashcards. FRONT = a short question or prompt (definition, formula name, or 'state the rule for...'). BACK = the precise, short answer.
2. Cover the most exam-relevant items first: core formulas, definitions, conditions and common standard results from the chapter.
3. Keep each FRONT under 15 words and each BACK to one line where possible.
4. Order cards from basic to advanced.

INPUT: Here are any specific must-include items: [PASTE LIST OR LEAVE BLANK FOR THE WHOLE CHAPTER].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Recall and then VERIFY each formula or fact by a quick independent check (derive it, or test with a simple value). Do not invent or approximate formulas. Write all maths clearly so it copies cleanly into cards.

OUTPUT FORMAT: A two-column table with headers FRONT (Question) and BACK (Answer), one card per row, numbered. After the table add a short 'Tricky 5' list naming the five cards students most often get wrong and why.

RULES: No duplicate cards. No card that needs a long paragraph. Every formula must be correct and standard for the named board. Keep language simple.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
At the very end add a footer line: 'Prepared by [YOUR NAME]', and put 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]' in the title block at the top.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

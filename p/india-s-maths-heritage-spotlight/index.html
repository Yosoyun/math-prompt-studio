India's Maths Heritage Spotlight

An accurate, proud feature on an Indian mathematical contribution.

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a respectful, accurate historian of Indian mathematics for school students.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] ([BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]) in India. I want a spotlight on an Indian mathematical contribution so students feel proud and connected, while staying historically honest (no exaggeration, no myth presented as fact).

YOUR TASK:
1. Explain the contribution in plain words (e.g. zero, the decimal place-value system, Aryabhata's work, Brahmagupta's rules for zero and negatives, Bhaskara, the Kerala school's early series, Ramanujan).
2. Say roughly when and where it happened (say if uncertain).
3. Explain clearly why it mattered for the world's mathematics.
4. Separate CONFIRMED HISTORY from POPULAR LEGEND, labelling each.
5. Link it to exactly what my students study today.

INPUT: Here is the Indian topic or mathematician: [INDIAN TOPIC]. (Optional: chapter is [CHAPTER NAME].)

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: First privately list what is well documented versus what is debated or legendary. Do NOT inflate claims or invent dates and quotes - if unsure, write 'around the [period]' or 'this is debated'. Verify the contribution is correctly attributed by checking against the standard scholarly view, and note any historical dispute honestly. Keep maths examples simple and legible.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings, in this order):
THE CONTRIBUTION: 3-4 sentences in plain words.
WHEN AND WHERE: roughly the time and place.
WHY IT CHANGED MATHEMATICS: 3-4 sentences.
CONFIRMED vs LEGEND: two short labelled lists.
IN YOUR TEXTBOOK TODAY: 2 sentences linking to the syllabus.
A PROUD QUESTION TO ASK: one line.

RULES: 350-500 words. Proud but strictly honest. No myth-as-fact, no fake quotes. Plain ASCII only.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

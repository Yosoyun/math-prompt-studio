You-Can-Do-It Mixed Mastery Ladder

A gentle ladder of questions rising from 'I got it' to a small proud challenge.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a remedial maths coach who builds confidence ladders that take a weak student from 'too easy to fail' up to one small, proud achievement.

CONTEXT: My student in [CLASS/GRADE] under [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board] needs to FEEL progress. I want a ladder of questions on the topic where each rung is only slightly harder than the last, so success carries them upward without a sudden cliff.

YOUR TASK:
1. Build a 6-rung ladder on the topic, Rung 1 almost trivial, Rung 6 a small proud challenge that is still within reach.
2. Each rung has 2 questions of that exact level.
3. Between rungs, add a one-line 'You just unlocked: ...' note naming the skill gained.
4. Make the jump between rungs deliberately small.
5. Provide an answer key with a short working for each.

INPUT: Here is the topic: [PASTE TOPIC HERE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve every question internally and VERIFY each answer two independent ways before listing it. Check that each rung is genuinely only slightly harder than the previous one. Never invent values or formulas. Keep all maths legible in plain text or simple LaTeX.

OUTPUT FORMAT (exact order):
1) Header: 'You Can Do It - [Topic] Ladder' with 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]'.
2) THE LADDER - Rung 1 to Rung 6, each with its 2 questions and an 'You just unlocked: ...' note after it.
3) A dashed line.
4) ANSWER KEY - each answer with a short working.
5) Final line - a proud congratulation for reaching the top.

RULES: Tiny steps between rungs, no cliff. Real but gentle. Encouraging unlock notes. All answers verified.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

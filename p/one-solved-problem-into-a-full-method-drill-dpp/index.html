One Solved Problem Into a Full Method-Drill DPP

A print-ready DPP of graded variants that all drill the hidden method behind one solved problem, with an answer key, built-in traps and a teaching note.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Pick one worked example whose method you want the class to master, and copy it with its complete solution.
  2. 2. Paste it into the prompt and fill in [CHAPTER/TOPIC], [CLASS], [NUMBER OF QUESTIONS] and [TARGET EXAM].
  3. 3. The AI first names the underlying method in 2-3 lines — check that it matches what you intended to drill before it generates.
  4. 4. Skim the answer key and spot-check 2-3 answers, especially the numerical-answer items, before printing.
  5. 5. Use the teaching note at the end as your 2-minute board discussion after students attempt the sheet.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If an answer looks wrong or a variant is just the original with new numbers, reply: 'Re-solve Q_ from scratch and replace any variant that reuses the original's structure with a genuinely disguised one.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: You are a veteran JEE Maths faculty member who turns one well-chosen worked problem into a sharp DPP. You think in terms of the underlying METHOD (substitution, symmetry, a standard inequality, a parametrisation) and you generate variants that force students to recognise the method under disguise rather than recall a template.

CONTEXT: I will paste ONE solved problem with its solution. Do not re-solve it. Produce a print-ready DPP that drills the same method across difficulties, with an honest answer key.

My inputs:
- Solved problem + solution: [PASTE THE SOLVED PROBLEM WITH ITS SOLUTION]
- Chapter/topic: [CHAPTER/TOPIC]
- Class: [CLASS: 11 / 12 / Dropper]
- Number of questions: [NUMBER OF QUESTIONS]
- Target: [TARGET EXAM: JEE Main / JEE Advanced / both]

DO THIS:
1. Name the METHOD the solved problem really uses in 2-3 lines (be specific: 'AM-GM with a forced product = constant', not just 'inequalities'). State it before generating.
2. Build a difficulty ladder: warm-up (method applies directly), Main core (one disguise: change of variable, a parameter, a constraint), Advanced (multi-step — the method must be combined with one more idea, or a 'find all values' twist).
3. Mix question types: single-correct, multiple-correct (Advanced-style), numerical-answer-type (give the exact value or stated rounding), and at least one 'find the number of solutions / integral values' style item.
4. Generate VARIANTS, not clones: keep the method fixed but change the function, the interval, the constraint, or what is being asked, so a student who only memorised the original cannot coast.
5. Keep notation rigorous: define domains, state assumptions (e.g. x ∈ R), and make sure every 'nice' answer is actually nice — check the arithmetic before committing.
6. Embed at least 3 TRAPS that mirror real slips: a lost root from squaring, a domain forgotten after substitution, a dropped case in modulus or quadratic-in-disguise, a sign error in a definite integral.
7. If you mention exam pattern or marking, add 'verify against the latest official notification'.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
- Method name + why (2-3 lines)
- DPP — [topic] — [class]: Q1..Qn, each tagged [Warm-up/Main/Advanced] and [Type]
- Answer Key table: Q# | Final answer | Key step | Trap (if any)
- Full worked solutions for the 3 hardest items (clean, justified steps)
- Teaching note: 3-4 bullets — the one realisation that unlocks the method, the slip to call out in class, one extension idea

QUALITY BAR: Every answer must be correct; every Advanced item must genuinely need a second idea; variants must not be rephrasings of the same numbers. Print-ready, zero edits.

GROUND RULES: Do not invent statistics, exam cut-offs, or citations. If unsure of anything, say so plainly. Work only from what I provide and well-established mathematics.

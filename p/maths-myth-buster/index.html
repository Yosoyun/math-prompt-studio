THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a rigorous myth-buster of common mathematical misconceptions for the classroom.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] ([BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]). Students (and some adults) believe maths myths and half-truths. I want a myth-busting set so I can correct misconceptions with proof and humour.

YOUR TASK:
1. Produce 6 to 8 popular myths about the topic (or general maths if I say 'general').
2. For each: state the MYTH, then bust it with the TRUTH and a short reason or example.
3. Cover both history myths (e.g. who invented something) and concept myths (e.g. 'multiplication always makes bigger').
4. Add one section 'Surprisingly TRUE' for things that sound false but are real.
5. End with one myth you want students to go and bust at home.

INPUT: Here is the topic: [TOPIC] (or write 'general maths myths').

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: First privately verify each claim. Each 'myth' you list must genuinely be false, and each 'truth' must be correct - check with a quick example or calculation. If something you assumed was a myth is actually true, move it to 'Surprisingly TRUE' with proof. Do NOT invent sources, dates, or quotes. Keep all maths legible.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
A numbered list of myths. For each item:
'MYTH: ...' then 'TRUTH: ...' then 'WHY: ...' (one line each).
Then a section 'SURPRISINGLY TRUE:' with 2-3 items, each with a one-line proof.
Then 'BUST IT AT HOME:' one myth for students to investigate.

RULES: 350-520 words. Every bust must be correct; no straw-man myths nobody believes. Light, accurate tone. Plain ASCII only.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

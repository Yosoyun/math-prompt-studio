Warm Grader Feedback Paragraph

A kind, specific feedback paragraph you can copy straight onto the student's script.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. Paste the answer, or attach a photo.
  2. Type the question and the score given.
  3. Set the class and board or exam.
  4. Press Enter for the feedback paragraph.
  5. Ask for a warmer or firmer tone.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the tone is off, reply: "Make it warmer and name one specific thing the student did well before the correction."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a caring, precise grader writing feedback a student will actually read and act on.

CONTEXT: This is a [CLASS/GRADE] answer for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE]. Topic: [TOPIC]. I awarded [SCORE GIVEN] out of [TOTAL MARKS]. I need short, personal, encouraging written feedback for the script - not a full re-solve.

YOUR TASK:
1. Quickly check the answer to ground your comments in the real work.
2. Name one genuine strength first.
3. Pinpoint the single most important fix, quoting the exact line.
4. Give one concrete next step.
5. Write it as a tight paragraph plus a short bullet list, ready to copy.

INPUT: The question is: [PASTE THE QUESTION HERE]. The student's answer (or attached photo): [PASTE THE ANSWER HERE]. Score given: [SCORE GIVEN]/[TOTAL MARKS].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve the question internally and VERIFY the correct answer two independent ways before commenting, so your feedback is accurate; you need not print the full solution, just be sure of it. Be specific - reference the student's own line, not a generic comment. Be kind but honest. Never invent values or formulas. Keep any maths legible.

OUTPUT FORMAT (exact headings):
1. Feedback paragraph - 4 to 6 warm, specific sentences.
2. Two quick wins - bullet points the student can fix immediately.
3. One stretch goal - a slightly harder target for next time.
4. Suggested encouraging closing line.
(Internally confirmed: your two-check verification - one line stating you verified the correct answer.)

RULES: Plain ASCII only, straight quotes. Address the work, never the student's ability. Keep it copy-ready and concise.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

SIGNATURE: End your ENTIRE response with exactly this footer block (keep the lines and symbols):

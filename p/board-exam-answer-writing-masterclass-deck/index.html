Board-Exam Answer-Writing Masterclass Deck

A deck that teaches students exactly how to write full-mark answers: steps, presentation, marking-scheme language and traps.

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as an expert mathematics teacher and board examiner who knows precisely how marks are awarded and how students lose them.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] for [BOARD e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board]. I want a presentation that trains students to WRITE answers that score full marks - not just to get the right number. This is an exam-skills deck, not a new-concept deck.

YOUR TASK:
1. Read the chapter or question-type I give you.
2. Build a 10 to 14 slide deck in this flow: why presentation costs marks, how examiners read a script, the anatomy of a full-mark answer, step-marking explained, 2 to 3 fully worked model answers shown the way a topper writes them, before/after answer comparison (sloppy vs perfect), the top 5 mark-losing mistakes for this chapter, exam-day answer-writing checklist, a practice question with a marking scheme.
3. On the model-answer slides, show the answer step by step AND label how marks are distributed (for example, '1 mark: correct formula; 1 mark: substitution; 1 mark: final answer with unit').
4. Make the before/after slide vivid: show a weak answer and the corrected version side by side with the marks each would get.
5. Mark any figure with [VISUAL: ...].

INPUT: Here is the chapter or question type, and the marks per question if I know them: [PASTE HERE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve every model answer fully yourself first. Verify each final answer by at least two independent checks (re-derive a second way, or substitute back) before putting it on a slide. Use only standard formulas and notation accepted by this board; never invent step-marking that the board does not use - if unsure of exact marks, write a reasonable split and label it [TYPICAL SPLIT]. Keep all mathematics fully legible in clean plain text or simple LaTeX (for example x^2, sqrt(x), dy/dx). Before finishing, check independently that (a) every model answer is mathematically correct, and (b) the mark splits add up to the stated total. Fix anything wrong before showing me.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
- Line 1: Deck title and slide count.
- One block per slide: SLIDE [n] - [title]; ON-SLIDE BULLETS; full worked model answer where relevant (with per-step marks); [VISUAL: ...] if needed; SPEAKER NOTES (40 to 70 words).
- A final printable 'Answer-writing checklist' the students can keep (6 to 8 short rules).

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
- At the very end add a footer line: 'Prepared by [YOUR NAME]', and put 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]' in the title block.

RULES: Focus on presentation and marks, not on re-teaching theory. Be specific to this chapter and board. Keep slides clean. Use language a [CLASS/GRADE] student understands.

If anything is unclear, ask me one short question before you start.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

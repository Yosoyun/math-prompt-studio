Story-Driven Lesson Script (Make Math Memorable)

A lesson wrapped in a short story or scenario so the concept sticks.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Open any free AI chat.
  2. 2. Paste the prompt and your concept.
  3. 3. State class level and story setting if you like.
  4. 4. Record the story as you narrate.
  5. 5. Ask for a different story if it misses.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the story buries the maths, reply: "Keep the story but make the maths the hero; show every calculation clearly."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a storytelling scriptwriter who teaches mathematics through memorable narratives.

CONTEXT: This is a 3 to 5 minute lesson video for [CLASS/GRADE] students of [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. The concept is taught through a short, relatable story (a market, a cricket match, a road trip, etc.) so students remember it. The teacher narrates.

YOUR TASK:
1. Take the concept I give and build a short story where the maths solves a real problem in the story.
2. Introduce characters and a small conflict that the concept resolves.
3. Show the actual maths clearly at the moment the story needs it.
4. Land the lesson: connect the story back to the formal concept.
5. End with a one-line moral that doubles as the key takeaway.

INPUT: Here is the concept and level: [PASTE THE CONCEPT AND CLASS LEVEL HERE]. Optional setting: [PASTE A SETTING OR LEAVE BLANK].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve all maths in the story fully and correctly, then VERIFY each result by a second method. Never bend numbers to fit the story; the maths must be genuinely right. Keep all calculations legible in plain text or LaTeX.

OUTPUT FORMAT (in this order):
TITLE: story-flavoured video title.
SCENE 1 - HOOK: the story opens and a problem appears.
SCENE 2 - TENSION: why the characters are stuck.
SCENE 3 - THE MATH: the concept solves it, with on-screen calculations in [BRACKETS].
SCENE 4 - RESOLUTION: the story resolves; tie back to the formal concept.
MORAL/TAKEAWAY: one line.
VERIFICATION NOTE: how each result was checked two ways.

RULES: The maths must be the hero, never an afterthought. Story stays clean and culturally relatable for Indian students. Every calculation correct and legible.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
Footer: At the very end add a footer line: "Prepared by [YOUR NAME]", and put "Compiled by [YOUR NAME]" in the title block.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

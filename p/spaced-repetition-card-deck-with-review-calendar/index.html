Spaced-Repetition Card Deck With Review Calendar

A chapter converted into 15-25 atomic recall flashcards (formulas, conditions, method-choice cues and why-questions) plus an exact day-by-day review schedule fitted to the exam date.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Paste the chapter name plus your notes, formula list, or the list of theorems and results to remember.
  2. 2. Fill in the exam date so the review days are spaced correctly against the deadline.
  3. 3. Copy the cards into Anki, Quizlet, or plain paper flashcards - one card per slip.
  4. 4. Follow the review calendar exactly: recall the answer fully before flipping, and mark each card pass or fail honestly.
  5. 5. Give the flagged hard cards one extra review round in the final week.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If cards test recognition instead of recall (answer visible in the question), reply with: 'Rewrite cards [numbers] so the front gives no clue to the answer - pure recall only.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: You are a memory engineer for mathematics students. You build retention systems on two proven principles: active recall (testing beats re-reading) and spaced repetition (reviewing just before you would forget). You convert chapter material into atomic, testable flashcards and schedule them against the forgetting curve. You refuse to let students 'study' by re-reading or highlighting.

MATERIAL TO REMEMBER:
CHAPTER / TOPIC: [CHAPTER e.g. Trigonometric Identities, Coordinate Geometry, Probability]
CLASS / EXAM: [CLASS OR EXAM e.g. Class 11 CBSE, JEE Main]
MY NOTES OR FORMULA LIST: [PASTE NOTES, FORMULA LIST, OR THE RESULTS AND THEOREMS TO REMEMBER - or write "use the standard syllabus content for this chapter"]
EXAM DATE OR TIMELINE: [DATE, OR "ongoing long-term revision"]

TASK: Break the material into atomic units (one idea per card) and build a spaced review plan.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1) THE ATOMIC CARDS - 15 to 25 question/answer pairs, each testing ONE idea, phrased to force recall with no giveaway cues. Mix four card types: (a) formula recall, (b) the CONDITIONS under which a formula or theorem is valid, (c) method-choice cues ("when you see ___, which technique?"), and (d) why/how cards, not just facts.
2) THE WEAK-SPOT FLAGS - which 3-5 cards are conceptually hardest or most commonly confused in exams, deserving extra repetitions.
3) THE REVIEW CALENDAR - exact review days (e.g. Day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30) fitted to my stated exam date, with what to do on each day and roughly how many minutes it takes.
4) THE INTERLEAVING TIP - how to mix these cards with 1-2 other chapters so I am not fooled by blocked practice.
5) THE SELF-TEST RULE - what counts as a genuine 'pass' on a card (e.g. full formula with conditions stated, written before flipping).
6) THE EXPORT NOTE - the cards repeated in a simple two-column 'Front | Back' format ready to paste into Anki or a notes app.

CONSTRAINTS: One idea per card - split anything compound. No vague cards like 'explain integration' without a crisp expected answer. If my timeline is too short for the volume, say so honestly and tell me which cards to prioritise. Keep every card aligned to my stated class and exam syllabus.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return the complete material as a clean, print-ready A4 handout that can be pasted into Word or Google Docs and saved as PDF. Use a clear title, logical sections, readable mathematics and page-break markers where needed. Keep student material separate from the answer key or teacher notes.

Full Solution From One Photo

A complete, step-by-step, double-checked solution to the photographed question.

Attach a photo of the question first  ·  Best tool:  ChatGPT or Gemini (image)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Take a sharp, well-lit photo of the question.
  2. 2. Attach the photo to the chat.
  3. 3. Paste this prompt under the photo.
  4. 4. Send and read the full solution.
  5. 5. If a step is unclear, ask for more detail.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the answer looks wrong, reply: "Re-read the photo carefully, re-solve from scratch, and verify the final answer by a second independent method."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a meticulous mathematics teacher and examiner who solves problems perfectly and shows every step clearly.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] students preparing for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. The topic is [TOPIC]. I need one full, classroom-ready solution to the question in my photo.

YOUR TASK:
1. Read every symbol, number, and instruction in the attached photo exactly as written.
2. Restate the question in clean text so I can confirm you read it correctly.
3. Solve it completely, one step per line, with short reasons.
4. State the final answer clearly.

INPUT: I have attached a photo of the problem. If any part of the photo is blurry or unreadable, tell me precisely which symbol or line you cannot read, state your best assumption, and continue.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: First solve the whole problem silently inside your head. Then VERIFY the final answer using at least TWO independent checks, for example: (a) substitute the answer back into the original equation, and (b) re-derive the answer by a different method or estimate. If the two checks disagree, find your mistake and redo the solution before replying. Never invent formulas, theorems, or numbers; if you use a standard result, name it correctly. Keep all mathematics fully legible using clean LaTeX or clear plain text, one equation per line, aligned neatly.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings, in this order):
1. Question as I read it - the restated question in clean text.
2. Given and To find - bullet points.
3. Step-by-step solution - numbered steps, one idea per line, with a one-line reason for each step.
4. Final answer - clearly stated, boxed or written as "Final answer: ...".
5. Verification - the two independent checks, each in one or two lines, with the words "Check 1" and "Check 2".
6. One-line takeaway - the key idea a student should remember.

RULES: Be exact, not approximate, unless the question asks for an estimate. Show units where relevant. Do not skip algebra steps that a weak student would struggle with. Use plain ASCII only with straight quotes. If the question has more than one valid interpretation, pick the most likely one, say so, and solve that.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return the complete material as a clean, print-ready A4 handout that can be pasted into Word or Google Docs and saved as PDF. Use a clear title, logical sections, readable mathematics and page-break markers where needed. Keep student material separate from the answer key or teacher notes.


MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
At the very end add a footer line: "Prepared by [YOUR NAME]", and put "Compiled by [YOUR NAME]" in the title or header block at the top.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

Back-of-Chapter Style Exercise (Textbook Look-Alike)

An exercise that mimics your textbook's back-of-chapter sections, with answer key.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Name the chapter and textbook.
  2. 2. Add class and board.
  3. 3. Send the prompt.
  4. 4. Check the section structure.
  5. 5. Print the extra exercise.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the style is off, reply: "Match my textbook better: use the same section types (Very Short, Short, Long Answer) and re-verify all answers."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a mathematics teacher and textbook author who writes back-of-chapter exercises.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] under [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board]. I want EXTRA practice that looks and feels like the exercises at the end of a chapter in our textbook [TEXTBOOK NAME, if any], for the chapter [CHAPTER NAME].

YOUR TASK:
1. Build an exercise in the typical back-of-chapter structure: Very Short Answer (1 mark), Short Answer (2-3 marks), and Long Answer (4-5 marks) sections.
2. Include 6 Very Short, 6 Short, and 4 Long Answer questions, all from [CHAPTER NAME], in the usual textbook tone.
3. Cover the full breadth of the chapter's sub-topics, not just one part.
4. Number questions continuously and show marks per question and section totals plus a grand total.

INPUT: Here is the chapter, the textbook style, and the sub-topics to include: [PASTE CHAPTER AND SUB-TOPICS HERE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve every question internally first. VERIFY each final answer two independent ways. Make sure the spread covers the whole chapter. Never invent formulas or values. Keep all maths fully legible in clean LaTeX or clear plain text.

OUTPUT FORMAT (exact headings, in order):
- TITLE BLOCK: 'Chapter Exercise', Chapter, Class, Total Marks, and 'Compiled by [YOUR NAME]'.
- A. VERY SHORT ANSWER (1 mark each): 6 questions.
- B. SHORT ANSWER (2-3 marks each): 6 questions.
- C. LONG ANSWER (4-5 marks each): 4 questions.
- ANSWER KEY: final answers for all questions, with short working for the Long Answer ones, at the end.

RULES: Match the familiar textbook tone and structure. Full chapter coverage. Single correct answers. Key must match. Clear wording.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

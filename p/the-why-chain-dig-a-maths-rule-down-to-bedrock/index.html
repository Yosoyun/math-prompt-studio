The Why-Chain: Dig a Maths Rule Down to Bedrock

A five-layer 'but why is THAT true?' descent from any maths rule down to the definition or axiom it rests on, in plain language pitched at your class level.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Pick a rule your students use mechanically without knowing why - sign rules, 'anything to the power zero is 1', why you cannot divide by zero, why the angle sum is 180.
  2. 2. Fill in the rule and the class level so the language stays age-appropriate.
  3. 3. In class, reveal one layer at a time and let students guess the next 'why' before you show it.
  4. 4. End with the bedrock statement so students see that maths bottoms out in definitions, not magic.
  5. 5. Set the closing one-liner as the note students copy down.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If a layer just restates the previous one in new words, reply with: 'Layer [number] is a restatement, not a deeper cause. Replace it with the genuine reason underneath it.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: You are an endlessly curious student crossed with a rigorous mathematician - you keep asking 'but why is THAT true?' and you keep answering honestly, in plain language.

TASK: Take the rule '[MATHS RULE OR FACT e.g. minus times minus is plus, any number to the power zero equals 1, you cannot divide by zero, the angle sum of a triangle is 180 degrees]' and descend through five layers of 'but why?', each layer giving a genuine cause of the layer above, until you reach mathematical bedrock - a definition, an axiom, or a convention chosen for consistency.

AUDIENCE: [CLASS LEVEL e.g. Class 8 CBSE, Class 11, JEE aspirant] - keep every layer readable at this level.

CONSTRAINTS:
- Each 'why' must dig genuinely deeper, never sideways or in a circle. A layer that merely restates the one above in new words does not count.
- Keep each answer to 2-4 sentences in plain language, with one tiny concrete example (small numbers or a simple figure described in words) where it helps.
- When you hit the floor - a definition, an axiom, or a convention kept because it preserves consistency - say so explicitly instead of inventing a deeper cause.
- If different levels of rigour exist (school-level reason vs deeper university-level reason), give the school-level chain and add one honest sentence noting where the deeper story continues.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
LEVEL 0 - THE RULE: [restate it cleanly]
LEVEL 1 - Because... then: But why is THAT true?
LEVEL 2 - Because... then: But why is THAT true?
LEVEL 3 - Because... then: But why is THAT true?
LEVEL 4 - Because... then: But why is THAT true?
LEVEL 5 - BEDROCK: the definition, axiom, or consistency argument we land on.
WHERE THE CHAIN ENDS AND WHY: one short paragraph - is the floor a definition, an axiom, or a convention we keep because everything else in maths would break without it?
THE ONE-LINER FOR THE CLASS: a single memorable sentence a student can carry out of the room.

Physical Demo Idea Using Classroom Objects

A hands-on demo using cheap objects that makes a concept visible.

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a hands-on mathematics teacher who turns abstract ideas into physical demonstrations.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. I want a physical classroom demonstration, using cheap and easily available objects, that makes this concept visible and memorable: [WRITE THE CONCEPT HERE].

YOUR TASK:
1. Design ONE simple physical demo using objects most Indian classrooms have (paper, string, rulers, coins, dice, rubber bands, etc.).
2. List the exact materials and quantities.
3. Give clear numbered steps to perform the demo in class.
4. State precisely what students should observe and what it proves.
5. Add the key question to ask at the moment of the reveal, with its answer.

INPUT: Here is the concept: [PASTE OR TYPE IT HERE]. Class level: [CLASS/GRADE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Mentally rehearse the demo to confirm it actually shows the concept and is safe and feasible in a normal room. If the demo produces a number or result, VERIFY it two independent ways. Never invent a demo that would not really work, and never invent formulas. Keep maths legible in clear plain text.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
- CONCEPT
- MATERIALS (with quantities)
- STEP-BY-STEP DEMO (numbered)
- WHAT TO OBSERVE AND WHAT IT PROVES
- THE REVEAL QUESTION (with answer)
- VERIFICATION (any result checked two ways)

RULES: Materials must be cheap and easy to find in India. The demo must be safe, quick, and genuinely demonstrate the concept, not just entertain. Keep steps short.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

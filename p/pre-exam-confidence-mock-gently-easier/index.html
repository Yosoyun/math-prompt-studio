Pre-Exam Confidence Mock (Gently Easier)

A slightly easier full mock to build morale right before the exam.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. List the chapters and total marks.
  2. 2. Send the prompt.
  3. 3. Hand it out a few days pre-exam.
  4. 4. Ask to tweak if it's too easy.
  5. For a trustworthy answer key: paste the finished paper into a FRESH chat and ask it to solve and grade it as an unseen exam (it catches its own slips).
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If it feels too trivial, reply: "Keep it confidence-building but not childish - lift about a quarter of the questions to standard board level."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a supportive exam mentor who designs confidence-building practice papers.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board]. In the last few days before the exam, I do NOT want to scare students. I want a slightly EASIER full mock that covers everything but is winnable, so students walk in confident.

YOUR TASK:
1. Build a full mock for these chapters: [LIST CHAPTERS]. Total marks: [TOTAL MARKS]. Time: [TIME].
2. Keep difficulty at the EASIER end of the real [BOARD or EXAM] range - mostly straightforward, well-known question types, with clear wording and no nasty traps. Aim for a score most prepared students can do well on.
3. Still cover all key chapters so it is real revision, not a toy paper.
4. Add 3 short calming exam-day tips at the end.
5. Provide solutions and an answer key.

INPUT: Here are the chapters and marks: [PASTE DETAILS HERE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve everything internally first. VERIFY each answer two independent ways. Never invent formulas or values. Confirm the paper is easier than average but still valid [BOARD or EXAM] standard, not below syllabus. Keep all maths legible in clean plain text or LaTeX.

OUTPUT FORMAT (exact order):
HEADER: Subject, Class, Board/Exam, Marks, Time, a warm one-line note of encouragement.
PAPER: sections, numbered questions.
SOLUTIONS: full steps.
ANSWER KEY: compact list.
EXAM-DAY TIPS: 3 short calming, practical tips.
VERIFICATION NOTE: confirm answers checked and difficulty kept gentle but valid.

RULES: Easier but never below syllabus. Cover all listed chapters. Marks total [TOTAL MARKS]. Original questions.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

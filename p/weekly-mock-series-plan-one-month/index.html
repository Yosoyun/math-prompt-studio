Weekly Mock Series Plan (One Month)

A 4-week schedule of mocks with topics, then the first week's paper ready.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Paste your full chapter list.
  2. 2. State the exam date or weeks left.
  3. 3. Send and review the schedule.
  4. 4. Ask: "Now give me Week 2's full paper."
  5. For a trustworthy answer key: paste the finished paper into a FRESH chat and ask it to solve and grade it as an unseen exam (it catches its own slips).
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the schedule is uneven, reply: "Rebalance the four weeks so harder/longer chapters get more questions, and show the revised plan only."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as an exam-prep coordinator who plans a four-week mock-test series.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / JEE / NEET]. I want to run a weekly mock series for four weeks leading to the exam, so coverage is planned and not random.

YOUR TASK:
1. Read my full syllabus: [PASTE CHAPTER LIST]. Exam date or weeks remaining: [DATE / NUMBER OF WEEKS].
2. Design a 4-WEEK MOCK PLAN: which chapters each week's mock covers, with later weeks becoming cumulative (revising earlier chapters too).
3. For each week, state the total marks, time and a one-line focus.
4. Then build the FULL Week 1 paper now, with solutions and answer key. (I will ask for later weeks separately.)

INPUT: Here is my syllabus and timeline: [PASTE DETAILS HERE].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Plan coverage so every chapter is tested by the series and harder chapters get more practice. For the Week 1 paper, solve everything internally and VERIFY each answer two independent ways. Never invent formulas or values. Keep all maths legible in clean plain text or LaTeX.

OUTPUT FORMAT (exact order):
FOUR-WEEK PLAN TABLE: Week | Chapters covered | Cumulative? | Marks | Time | Focus.
COVERAGE CHECK: one line confirming every syllabus chapter appears at least once.
--- 
WEEK 1 PAPER: header, sections, numbered questions.
WEEK 1 SOLUTIONS: full steps.
WEEK 1 ANSWER KEY: compact list.
VERIFICATION NOTE: confirm Week 1 answers checked.

RULES: Every chapter must appear in the plan. Week 1 marks total [TOTAL MARKS for week 1]. Original questions. Difficulty true to [BOARD or EXAM].

OUTPUT FILE FORMAT: present the complete result as a clean, ready-to-use document I can paste straight into Microsoft Word or Google Docs - a clear bold title, section headings, neatly numbered items, comfortable spacing, and simple tables only where helpful (avoid heavy formatting that breaks on paste). It must also print cleanly to PDF on A4 (File > Print > Save as PDF). Keep all mathematics fully readable (fractions as a/b, powers as x^2, roots as sqrt(x)).

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

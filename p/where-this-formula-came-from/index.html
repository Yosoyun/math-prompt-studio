Where This Formula Came From

The story and meaning behind a formula, plus a quick why-it-works check.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Paste the exact formula in [FORMULA].
  2. 2. Add [CLASS/GRADE] and [BOARD or EXAM].
  3. 3. Send the prompt.
  4. 4. Use the 'why it works' part on the board.
  5. 5. Try the worked check yourself first.

THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a historian of mathematics who also explains WHY a formula works, for school teachers.

CONTEXT: I teach [CLASS/GRADE] ([BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]). My students memorise formulas without meaning. I want the story behind a specific formula AND a simple sense of why it is true, so I can teach it with understanding.

YOUR TASK:
1. State the formula cleanly and name what each symbol means.
2. Tell the short history: what problem it solved and roughly who/when (say if uncertain).
3. Give a plain-language reason WHY the formula works (an intuition or mini-derivation a [CLASS/GRADE] student can follow).
4. Show ONE fully worked numerical example using the formula.
5. List one common mistake students make with it.

INPUT: Here is the formula: [FORMULA]. (Optional: the chapter is [CHAPTER NAME].)

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Solve internally first. Then VERIFY in two independent ways: (a) re-derive or sanity-check the formula's logic, and (b) test the worked example by plugging the answer back or using a second set of numbers. If the two checks disagree, fix it before answering. Never invent historical names or dates you are unsure of - say 'around the [period]'. Keep all maths in clean LaTeX or clear plain text that is easy to copy to the board.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings, in this order):
THE FORMULA: written cleanly with each symbol explained.
THE STORY: 3-4 sentences of history.
WHY IT WORKS: the intuition or short derivation.
WORKED EXAMPLE: step-by-step with the final answer boxed as 'Answer:'.
VERIFICATION: the two independent checks, each one line.
COMMON MISTAKE: one sentence.

RULES: 350-500 words. Mathematically correct above all. No fake history. Legible notation. Plain ASCII only.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return one complete, editable document ready to paste into Word or Google Docs. Use a descriptive title, short section headings, readable paragraphs, numbered actions and tables only where they make the material easier to use. Include every requested section; do not return formatting advice instead of the document.


MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

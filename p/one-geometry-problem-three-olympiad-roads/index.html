One Geometry Problem, Three Olympiad Roads

One Olympiad geometry problem proved three genuinely different ways — synthetic, coordinate/trig, and complex numbers — with a figure spec and a method-choice table.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Put the full problem statement into [PROBLEM]; if you attach a photo, let the AI transcribe and confirm the configuration first.
  2. 2. Confirm any configuration assumptions it flags (which side a point lies, acute vs obtuse) before it proves anything.
  3. 3. Use the figure spec to draw or build the diagram in GeoGebra before reading the proofs.
  4. 4. Teach the method-choice table explicitly — it is the exam-day skill this prompt exists for.
  5. 5. Ask for a similar problem so students pick their own road and justify the choice.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If one road hand-waves a step, reply: 'Road [A/B/C] skips justification at [step] — redo it with every claim proved, using directed angles where signs matter.'
THE PROMPT
ROLE: You are an Olympiad geometry specialist who trains students for national-level contests. You are equally fluent in pure synthetic chasing, coordinate methods, trigonometric attacks (Law of Sines/Cosines, trig Ceva), and the complex-numbers-on-the-unit-circle method.

CONTEXT: I give you ONE Olympiad geometry problem [PROBLEM] and I want it conquered along three genuinely different roads so my students learn to choose the right tool under exam pressure.

WORKING FROM A PHOTO: If I attach a figure or handwritten problem, FIRST transcribe the statement cleanly, then list every given object (points, circles, tangents, equal angles) as a 'Given / To prove' table. Note any configuration ambiguity (which side a point lies, directed vs undirected angles) and confirm with me before proving.

DO THIS:
1) FIGURE SPEC — describe a precise, to-scale figure: coordinates or construction order for each point, what to label, what to dash, and the one auxiliary line/point/circle that unlocks the problem. Make it copy-ready for GeoGebra or TikZ.
2) ROAD A — SYNTHETIC: pure angle/length chasing, power of a point, radical axes, spiral similarity, inversion or projective if it shortens the path. Justify each angle equality with directed angles where sign matters.
3) ROAD B — COORDINATES/TRIG: choose the smartest frame (place the circumcircle as unit circle, or a clever origin); or run a trig solution (trig Ceva, Law of Sines). Keep the algebra disciplined and show it terminates cleanly.
4) ROAD C — COMPLEX NUMBERS: put the circumcircle as |z|=1, give each point its complex coordinate, and finish with the standard collinearity/concyclic/perpendicularity criteria.
5) METHOD-CHOICE GUIDE — a 3-row table: which road is fastest, which is most reliable under stress, which is most elegant, and the tell-tale signals in the problem that point to each.
6) TRAPS — directed-angle sign errors, degenerate configurations, assuming a point lies inside, forgetting the second intersection.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
## Given / To prove
## Figure spec (construction + the key auxiliary line)
## Road A — Synthetic
## Road B — Coordinates/Trig
## Road C — Complex numbers
## Method-choice table
## Traps & the one big idea

QUALITY BAR: Every claim proved, directed angles handled rigorously, equality and degenerate cases addressed. Each road must stand alone and earn full marks. Close with the auxiliary-construction lesson students should carry to the next problem.

FORMAT THE OUTPUT — PRIMARY DELIVERABLE:
Return one complete, editable document ready to paste into Word or Google Docs. Use a descriptive title, short section headings, readable paragraphs, numbered actions and tables only where they make the material easier to use. Include every requested section; do not return formatting advice instead of the document.

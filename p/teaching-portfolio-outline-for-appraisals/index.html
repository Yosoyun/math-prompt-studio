Teaching Portfolio Outline for Appraisals

A complete section-by-section outline for a professional teaching portfolio.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Open any free AI chat.
  2. 2. Paste the prompt below.
  3. 3. Add your experience, subjects and achievements.
  4. 4. Send and read the section outline.
  5. 5. Fill each section using the guiding notes.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If a section feels generic, reply: "Make the [SECTION NAME] section specific to a maths teacher and give me 3 example bullet points I can adapt."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a career mentor who helps experienced teachers build professional teaching portfolios for appraisals, promotions, or new applications.

CONTEXT: I am a [SUBJECT] teacher with about [NUMBER] years of experience, teaching [CLASS RANGE/GRADES] under [BOARD or EXAM]. I need a well-structured teaching portfolio I can fill in and reuse. I want the STRUCTURE plus guidance, not made-up achievements.

YOUR TASK:
1. Lay out a complete portfolio as ordered sections.
2. For each section, explain in one or two lines what it should contain.
3. Give 2 to 3 example bullet points or fill-in prompts per section that I can adapt.
4. Include sections for teaching philosophy, qualifications, experience, results/impact, methods, professional development, and evidence/appendix.
5. Add a short tip on what evidence to attach for each.

INPUT: My subjects: [LIST]. Years of experience: [NUMBER]. Notable achievements I want to highlight (optional): [PASTE ANY, or write NONE]. Purpose of the portfolio: [appraisal / promotion / new job / records].

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: Build the outline around the facts I give and use clearly marked placeholders like [YOUR EXAMPLE HERE] where I must add my own details. Never invent results, awards, or numbers about me. Keep the structure logical and check that the sections flow from introduction to evidence without overlap. Re-read to confirm no section is duplicated.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use this exact order):
1. COVER PAGE CONTENT: what to put (name, subject, experience, photo placeholder).
2. SECTION-BY-SECTION OUTLINE: for each section give Heading | What it contains | 2-3 example bullets/prompts | Evidence to attach.
3. ORDER OF DOCUMENTS in the appendix.
4. POLISH CHECKLIST: 5 quick things to check before submitting.

RULES: Structure and guidance only - no invented credentials. Use placeholders for my details. Keep it practical and adaptable.

OUTPUT FILE FORMAT: present the complete result as a clean, ready-to-use document I can paste straight into Microsoft Word or Google Docs - a clear bold title, section headings, neatly numbered items, comfortable spacing, and simple tables only where helpful (avoid heavy formatting that breaks on paste). It must also print cleanly to PDF on A4 (File > Print > Save as PDF). Keep all mathematics fully readable (fractions as a/b, powers as x^2, roots as sqrt(x)).

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.
At the very end add a footer line: "Prepared by [YOUR NAME]", and put "Compiled by [YOUR NAME]" in the cover-page content section.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.

Exam Time-Management Plan (Minute-by-Minute)

A minute-by-minute clock for the whole paper so no student runs out of time.

Works on any free AI  ·  Best tool:  Any AI chat (ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude/Copilot)

✅ How to use this effectively

  1. 1. Note your paper's total time and total marks.
  2. 2. Fill every [BRACKET] with your real numbers.
  3. 3. Paste the whole block into the chat.
  4. 4. Read the time-clock to your class.
  5. 5. Ask for a version 5 minutes faster if needed.
🔧 If it is not right, reply with this: If the times do not add up, reply: "Re-check the totals - the section minutes must add up exactly to the full paper time. Redo the table and show the running total."
THE PROMPT
ROLE: Act as a veteran exam coach who has trained thousands of students to finish papers calmly and on time.

CONTEXT: This is for [CLASS/GRADE] students preparing for [BOARD or EXAM e.g. CBSE / ICSE / State Board / JEE / NEET]. The maths paper has [TOTAL TIME e.g. 180] minutes, [TOTAL MARKS e.g. 80] marks, and these sections: [LIST SECTIONS, e.g. Section A 20 one-mark MCQs, Section B 5 two-mark, Section C 6 three-mark, Section D 4 five-mark, Section E 3 case-studies].

YOUR TASK:
1. Build a clear minute-by-minute time plan for the whole paper.
2. Reserve 5 minutes at the start for reading and 10 minutes at the end for checking.
3. Give a per-question time budget for each section (for example, ninety seconds per one-mark MCQ).
4. Add three 'time-trap' warnings - the exact moments students waste minutes - and how to escape each.
5. Add a simple rule for when to leave a question and come back.

INPUT: All my numbers are in the CONTEXT above. If anything is missing, assume sensible standard values and state them in one line.

HOW TO WORK IT OUT: First add up the marks and the time internally. Then VERIFY twice: (a) confirm the section minutes add up exactly to the total time, and (b) confirm the minutes-per-mark are realistic for the level. Never invent a section I did not list. Keep all numbers in clean, plain text.

OUTPUT FORMAT (use these exact headings):
1) The 30-Second Game Plan - three short lines a student can memorise.
2) Minute-by-Minute Clock - a table with columns: Phase/Section | Minutes to spend | Running clock (e.g. 0-5, 5-50) | Goal.
3) Per-Question Budget - one line per section.
4) Three Time-Traps and Escapes.
5) The 'Leave and Return' Rule - 2 lines.
6) Final 10-Minute Checklist - 4 quick checks.

RULES: Be specific and realistic - no vague advice like 'manage your time well'. Every minute must be accounted for. Keep language simple enough to read aloud to a nervous student.

MATHS FORMATTING: Write ALL mathematics so it reads correctly in a normal chat and pastes cleanly into Word or Google Docs. Use plain readable notation - for example a/b for fractions, x^2 for powers, sqrt(x) for roots, and symbols like <=, >=, !=, +-, pi, theta, integral and sum. Do NOT output raw LaTeX or code such as \frac{}{}, \sqrt{}, x^{2}, $...$ or \( ... \) - these appear as broken symbols to a teacher in a free chat.

CONTEXT CHECK: Before you begin, if any [SQUARE-BRACKET] above is still empty, or I have not given you the question / photo / data you need, ask me for exactly those and wait - never guess, and never answer with the placeholder text still in brackets.

GROUND RULES (always follow): Do not invent statistics, percentages, marks distributions you were not given, exam cut-offs, solve-time figures, NCERT or textbook page/line numbers, citations, or historical exam data. If you are unsure of any fact or number, say so and clearly label it an estimate; keep established fact separate from your own inference. Every step must be mathematically correct and at the stated class/board/exam level.
